Draft specification · RFC 0002

People, conversations
& shared context.

Intentional communication between people and their Ships, with explicit control over attention, information, and action.

20 September 2026 · Source baseline e917c3f4 · Proposal, not a release announcement

This document defines the whole social model. It includes existing pairing, messages, resources, and work requests, then specifies how profiles, an inbox, shared connections, and optional scoped assistance extend them. No social runtime changes or local verification have been performed for this document.

Status labels describe delivery stages. Every proposed behavior remains subject to review. Specific syscall names below are proposed contracts, not APIs available today. The product stages are distinct from federation wire versions.

00 / PRODUCT BOUNDARY

A contact book, an inbox, and context.

GSV lets a person publish a profile, approach another person, exchange durable messages and files, and optionally invite their Ship to help. Each space keeps authority over its own accounts, conversations, permissions, and data. A remote message is an input to consider; it is never a local syscall or a permission grant.

PeopleChoose who you know

Profiles, private aliases, contacts, shared connections, introductions, and explicit recommendations.

InboxChoose what gets attention

Accepted conversations and a separate queue for first contact. Private unread state, notification controls, mute, block, and reporting.

AssistanceChoose what a Ship may do

Ask your Ship about a thread, approve a draft, or later authorize an ordinary Process to help within a narrow scope.

There is no combined activity feed, global people index, automatic crawl of contacts-of-contacts, or algorithmic discovery. Browsing an individual profile or its deliberately shared connections is supported. Local search covers your own address book, visible shared records, and authorized conversation history.

01 / WHO SOMEONE IS

Identity survives a display-name change.

A space is an installation with a Kernel and local accounts. A social actor is one account's federated subject, authenticated by that space. A profile is the information that account deliberately publishes about itself. My spaces ownership, login credentials, email addresses, local numeric user IDs, and other accounts are not automatically public.

The public profile

FIRST RELEASE An eligible local account can publish one profile at https://<space>/@<username>. Here, username is a deliberately chosen public alias; it may match the login name, but publication does not expose the account directory. Multiple accounts can publish separate profiles in the same space.

Published fieldMeaning
Public alias and display nameAlias locates the profile; display name is descriptive and need not be unique.
About and avatarBounded plain text and an explicitly published immutable image reference. No arbitrary HTML, remote widgets, or tracking content.
Contact preferenceMessage requests open, invitation only, or closed. This is current intake policy, not proof of availability or willingness to perform work.
RepresentationExplain whether replies may come from the person's Ship. Individual messages still carry their own submission provenance.
Identity, revision, supported protocolMachine-readable metadata permits authenticated resolution and refresh without exposing private local IDs.

Publication starts off. The owner previews the fields before publishing. System/root accounts are not automatically published; standalone agent personas are outside this first release. A space may later nominate a profile as its visitor frontpage, but /@username works independently of the existing login and application routes.

Implementation: identity records, aliases, and profile publication

ActorRef = { shipId, subjectId } is the canonical federated key in this release. A local ownerUid maps to the existing federation_subjects.subject_id. Profile records add normalized unique alias, publication state, content revision, contact policy, and references to deliberately published media. Login usernames and public aliases have separate lifecycles.

Use a bounded lowercase ASCII alias grammar consistent with account-name usability, reject ambiguous encodings and path separators, and enforce uniqueness in Kernel SQLite. Renaming an alias updates the published locator without changing subject identity. Old aliases return a tombstone or an owner-authorized redirect; they are not silently reassigned to another identity. Display names and biography text can be Unicode.

The Kernel owns publication decisions. A durable publication job writes a minimal sanitized snapshot into installation-scoped R2. The Gateway serves that projection through a separate public route, with versioned content, ETags, a short bounded cache lifetime, and a revocation check for publication status. Cached content is public content only. Unpublishing invalidates the locator and serving projection; previously fetched copies cannot be recalled.

Contacts pin identities, not aliases. On a known identity/key mismatch, resolution requires explicit re-verification. The current implementation hashes the Ship public key into its ID and refuses an origin change; this RFC does not claim to have solved portable identity. Accounts resets create a new installation and a new social identity. Matching an old handle, email, or owner login never transfers relationships.

The relevant AT Protocol lesson is separating durable identity, readable handle, hosting, and keys. We retain native GSV federation and introduce no AT repository, relay, DID directory, or global identity dependency. AT Protocol identity reference.

02 / HOW PEOPLE CONNECT

Receiving, accepting, and saving are separate.

A contact is an account's private saved relationship with a pinned remote actor. A message request is a bounded first approach awaiting a decision. A transport relationship supplies authenticated delivery and generation fencing. None of these grants filesystem, machine, or agent authority.

ActionWhat changesWhat it does not imply
Visit a profileRead the explicitly published card.No contact is added and no message is sent.
Send an approachCreate one pending exchange at each end.No accepted relationship, model wake, or work commitment.
Accept conversationAuthorize this direct exchange and establish its transport rights.No requirement to save a contact, disclose connections, or enable automation.
Save contactAdd a private address-book entry with an optional local alias.No reciprocal endorsement or published edge.
Share connectionPublish a separately authorized relationship assertion to a defined audience.No transitive trust or permission inheritance.

Two ways to start

Private invitation EXISTS

A human creates an expiring, single-use pairing invitation and shares it intentionally. The other account explicitly accepts it. The paired identities and generation are pinned. Pending invitations can be listed and cancelled; revoking an established relationship is a separate action.

Public approach FIRST RELEASE

A visitor uses Message with my GSV, selects their own space/account, and composes on their own authenticated origin. The sending Kernel signs a first message to the published subject. The recipient sees it in Requests and chooses accept, decline, block, or report.

A first approach contains bounded text and sender identity only. Unknown senders cannot attach bytes, cause link previews, reserve a Process, or repeatedly fill the inbox with follow-up messages before acceptance. One unresolved approach per actor pair is shown as one item; retrying the same send intent cannot create another item.

Local controls

Implementation: relationship policy, first-contact state, and pairing recovery

Represent policy with explicit fields rather than a trust score: saved/not saved; communication allowed/revoked; actor block; notification policy; automation policy; and a monotonic policy revision. A durable block is keyed by owner and ActorRef, not by generation or URL, and therefore survives re-pair attempts.

First-contact state is pending → accepting → accepted, with terminal declined, withdrawn, expired, or blocked alternatives. The recipient owns acceptance/decline. The sender owns withdrawal intent. Acceptance remains visibly pending while transport establishment is incomplete. Duplicate and crossed operations preserve both participants' decisions instead of using wall-clock last-writer-wins.

Only after admission and quotas pass does the Kernel allocate an owner-scoped pending Conversation and append the first message. Its handler is optional: today handler_pid is required, so the public contract, registry migration, clients, and callers must support a contact conversation with no Process. The message body lives in that Conversation; the intake row retains IDs, bounded admission metadata, and recovery progress.

Acceptance reuses the invitation/pairing implementation's primitives: fresh private authorization material, hashed invite lookup, actor-bound durable attempts, pinned documents, and exact-generation recovery. The v2 handshake must bind the already approved sender, recipient, and approach ID; it may not publish a reusable invite or accept a different actor holding a setup artifact. Credential handoff uses the verified peer origin over HTTPS. Its lost-response and secret-retention design is a required wire-contract review before code, not permission to invent a new key exchange.

Accepting does not replace the first message or change its ID. Sender and recipient keep separate local conversation IDs, linked by an authenticated exchange ID. A sender cannot select the recipient's local uid, pid, conversation ID, storage partition, or installation ID.

Private invitations retain their current explicit human authorization. An invitation issuer can nominate an existing conversation or create one without publishing a profile. Pairing creates no personal-agent work. Explicit user assistance and separately scoped automation own any later agent admission.

03 / WHAT PEOPLE EXCHANGE

Durable conversations, honest delivery.

EXISTS Each participant owns a local canonical Conversation containing committed human-visible messages. Conversations survive Process replacement and deletion. Reasoning, tool calls, execution traces, and drafts remain in Process history; they are not federated automatically.

FIRST RELEASE A direct relationship has a default conversation. The data model permits additional explicitly opened topics between the same two actors; the first UI can expose only the default conversation. Reply references connect messages without inventing a separate Process or permission boundary for every reply.

SAM'S SHIP · replying for Sam
I found the setup guide you asked for. Sam can help with the remaining account question.
Delivered to your GSV · reply to your question · 1 explicitly shared resource
Visible stateWhat it means
SendingThe client has an optimistic message and a stable send-intent ID; durable local acknowledgement has not arrived.
QueuedThe sender's Kernel has durably accepted the message for delivery.
DeliveredThe remote Kernel has durably committed it to the intended request/conversation. It says nothing about human reading, agreement, or execution.
Retrying / failedShow inspectable delivery state and an explicit retry action. An ambiguous retry keeps the same intent ID.
Unread / read hereA private cursor for this human account, synchronized across its devices. Agent inspection never advances it.
Human read remotelyDEFERRED No public read receipts or presence are implied by the first release.

The composer feels immediate: insert the optimistic message, clear the submitted draft, and display a sending indicator. An acknowledgement reconciles the same message; failure preserves its content and permits retry or editing as a new intent. Reconnection merges committed IDs and delivery updates without duplicating messages.

Who sent it

Every v2 message preserves its represented actor and its submission path: human-submitted, Process-sent, or Process-prepared with a recorded human approval. The sending Kernel derives this from authenticated runtime context. Human-submitted describes the submission path, not a claim that no AI helped write the text. Old messages remain authorship unspecified.

A remote GSV authenticates its own assertion; it cannot cryptographically prove that a particular human was at a keyboard. Approving a draft binds the exact text, recipients, and attachments. Changing any of those requires a fresh approval.

Attachments and selected resources

EXISTS Messages carry immutable resource references with bounded metadata. The sender retains the exact revision and grants the recipient's current relationship generation access. Bytes stream lazily with cancellation and byte limits; a resource locator is not a bearer credential.

An accepted recipient can view a shared item or explicitly import it for independent retention. Revoking a grant stops future authorized fetches; it cannot erase a copy already imported. Sending a file does not expose its parent folder, future revisions, account home, or connected machine. First approaches are text-only.

Implementation: IDs, ordering, replies, and client synchronization

Keep distinct identifiers: local conversation ID; authenticated exchange/thread ID; transport generation; delivery ID; immutable origin message reference; and local sequence. Define origin references as { actor: ActorRef, messageId }, with the exchange binding recorded alongside them. Store a unique mapping to the receiver's local message/sequence. Existing delivery-derived local IDs remain valid; never fabricate a missing historical origin ID.

replyTo references an origin message in the same authorized exchange. Validate it against the local mapping, or show a bounded unresolved reference until reordered delivery arrives. It never grants access to another conversation. Local sequence orders the local conversation; remote wall-clock values are optional presentation claims and do not order authority or overwrite state.

Use the existing message.committed, conversation.changed, and correlated delivery signals. Add revisioned private view-state signals and a cursor-based catch-up path. A read cursor advances monotonically to an observed local sequence; an optional “mark unread” override is separate from that cursor. Marking read is an authenticated human-client action, not a side effect of history access.

Persist a stable client intent before sending, reconcile by idempotency key, and compare payload fingerprints. The same key with different text or resources fails. Automatic retries retain the same key; a deliberate resend with a new identity is visibly a new message. Client streaming is presentation only until the canonical commit. Remote federation sends committed messages, not partial reasoning or raw live Process output.

Extend the existing resource contract with conversation scope for restricted participants. The current maximum is 16 resources and 48 MiB total per message, with 32 KiB text. Preserve those limits until an intentional change. Native transfer, metadata validation, exact revision retention, cancellation, resource leases, and generation rechecks continue to own actual byte delivery.

Editing, remote redaction, reactions, group membership, typing indicators, and cross-party read receipts are deferred. Their future events must reference immutable message identities and define archive/search retention. Archiving or removing a contact in this release must not be described as deleting message data.

04 / REQUESTS TO DO SOMETHING

A work request is an offer, not a remote command.

EXISTS GSV already exchanges structured requests with kind, title, arbitrary bounded details, lifecycle state, participant direction, and revision. This represents an offer and status claims. It does not define measurable progress, a workflow, deliverable validation, or a general meaning of “done.”

The local responsibility ledger, r12y, separately owns execution tracking: assignee, blockers, check-in dates, deadlines, parent responsibilities, and resolution. The other party receives only explicitly shared request updates and results, not that private ledger or the Process history behind it.

ParticipantAuthorized statement
RequesterOffer a bounded task, withdraw the offer, or request stopping accepted work.
Recipient / performerAccept or reject the offer; report active work, a result, or confirmed cancellation.
Requester after a resultAcknowledge or dispute the reported outcome. A performer declaring completion does not imply requester satisfaction.
Either participant's ShipMake only the statements its human has authorized it to make. A remote kind string installs no code and grants no syscall.

Current correctness work is mandatory. The reviewed runtime checks state transitions but not participant roles, and crossed acceptance/cancellation can diverge. Fix role checks independently. Before exposing new v2 work controls, make unsettled outcomes explicit and recoverable.

Implementation: participant-owned updates and the limited v1 contract

Keep the original offer immutable. A v2 request ID includes its originating actor. Store participant-owned operation streams: the requester owns withdrawal intent, and the performer owns its decision and execution reports. Each operation has a stable ID, participant-local revision, and causal reference to the offer. The local Kernel serializes its own writer; incoming updates validate role, identity, generation, and operation identity.

This is a small deterministic state reducer, not a general CRDT or another task engine. Duplicate operations return the saved outcome. A missing revision schedules bounded reconciliation with the same authenticated peer; a conflict must not simply become a terminal delivery error while both sides display different agreements.

If withdrawal crosses acceptance, retain both facts and show “cancellation requested” until the performer reports what happened. A completion that already occurred remains a completion report; withdrawal cannot undo performed work. There is no last-write-wins rule based on remote time.

Update the local request projection and its r12y link in the same Kernel SQL transaction. Incoming offers awaiting human review are inbox items, not automatically accepted promises. Admitted follow-through can create a responsibility keyed by request ID; later changes update that record rather than spawning duplicate obligations.

For v1 peers, enforce actor roles on both local and inbound handlers. Preserve existing bounded fields and states, show delivery/settlement failures, and disable actions whose semantics cannot be represented safely. Do not quietly label a legacy local mutation as mutually settled. New first-contact messaging does not require a new work language.

DEFERRED A richer commitment model could add an expected outcome, due date, progress notes, milestones, result references, and separate acknowledgement. This RFC does not add generic percentage progress, a workflow DSL, automatic contract execution, or Jev-based authorization.

05 / CONTEXT FROM PEOPLE YOU CHOOSE

Shared connections, without a global graph.

FOLLOW-UP People may deliberately share selected connections, recommendations, and safety advisories. Your space receives updates from direct sources you choose and evaluates them locally. A mentioned person does not automatically become a subscribed source.

Shared recordMeaningPresentation
ConnectionBoth endpoints permit disclosure of this relationship to the defined audience.“Alice has shared a connection with Sam.”
RecommendationAn attributed statement by its publisher, optionally about a specific subject or skill. It is not a claim of reciprocal friendship.“Chen recommends Sam for Rust development.”
Safety advisoryAn attributed reason to exercise caution, with a date and optional deliberately shared evidence.“Alice shared a spam warning about this actor.”

Private blocking and muting are not automatically advisories. Contact membership is not an endorsement. Signing an assertion identifies its source; it does not establish the truth of the assertion. Connections and recommendations confer no Process permissions.

Surface shared context when viewing a person, considering a message request, or browsing a contact's chosen shared connections. Show the source and freshness. Never count hidden edges in “mutual connections,” expose hidden accounts through aggregate counts, or silently infer that an absent warning means someone is safe.

Introductions are deliberate messages

A person can ask a mutual contact for an introduction. The intermediary asks the proposed recipient whether they want it before forwarding private context. With consent, they send a selected introduction into the participants' separate conversations; each recipient still chooses whether to engage. This grants no automatic contact, shared resource access, group membership, or agent authority.

Implementation: assertions, visibility consent, subscriptions, and withdrawal

A shared assertion has assertionId, issuer ActorRef, subject ActorRef, kind, bounded text/category, revision, issued/expiry metadata, audience, provenance, and optional immutable evidence references. The publisher's private relationship rows remain separate. Updates and tombstones use issuer-owned monotonic revisions and stable IDs.

A shared connection also references approval from each endpoint. Each approval binds the exact actor pair, publisher, audience rule, revision, and expiry using the existing identity-signing primitives. The audience is named explicitly, for example “accepted contacts of Alice who subscribe to Alice's shared connections.” Accepting a conversation is not consenting to this audience. A newly broadened audience requires both endpoints' consent; arbitrary unsigned claims of the other person's permission do not qualify.

First implement audience-restricted sharing to authenticated direct contacts. Public publication of the complete connection set is deferred. Recommendations may point to an intentionally public profile, but cannot expose a private account or selected private evidence without the relevant disclosure rights.

Subscriptions are local, explicit, per source and record kind. A source authenticates the viewer and filters every sync page by current audience. Cursor tokens bind viewer, source, policy revision, and snapshot position. A snapshot followed by revision deltas prevents gaps; too-old cursors request a bounded resnapshot. The publisher maintains durable withdrawal deliveries for previous recipients so removing someone from the audience does not leave an indefinitely active cached assertion.

Use finite visibility leases. A revoked assertion disappears immediately at its owner and at recipients once the tombstone arrives; disconnected recipients stop displaying it at lease expiry. Source unavailability produces stale/unavailable context, not an invented current endorsement. No protocol can erase a copy someone already exported. Lease duration is a configurable, explicit privacy/availability tradeoff, initially capped at 24 hours for shared relationship display.

Store authorized incoming assertions in an owner-scoped local cache, indexed by subject identity and source. Enforce source, record-count, payload, and sync-work budgets. Query only this cache when showing request/profile context; do not ask every contact about each visitor. Removing a subscription removes its active projection and schedules bounded physical cleanup. Reports and locally retained evidence are separately owned records.

Start with visible warnings and explicit introduction actions. Automatically adopting a source's blocks is deferred until an owner opts into a clearly named policy with overrides and withdrawal handling. There is no recursive trust propagation, popularity-based allow rule, or hidden reputational score.

06 / THE PERSON AND THEIR SHIP

Agent involvement is an explicit permission.

The person's Ship can receive an explicitly admitted contact message and decide what to do with it. Federation still delivers ordinary Messages and bounded operations. It never imports another person's prompts as standing instructions or exposes raw Process activity.

Current cutover: contact conversations no longer expose a social helper or draft-review workflow. Each contact has a private Ship-attention switch; enabling it lets an incoming message create one bounded Ship responsibility, while sending authority and speaker attribution remain separate.

ModeBehaviorStage
Human conversationReceive and send without starting a Process. Requests produce an inbox item and optional human notification.FIRST RELEASE
Ship attentionEach contact can explicitly allow new messages to create one bounded Ship responsibility. Ship decides whether to read, delegate or reply.FIRST RELEASE
Scoped workAn ordinary Process may work within explicit conversation, resource, target and recipient grants. It is created only by an intentional owner action.FIRST RELEASE
Reply authorityReceiving a message never grants contact.send. A human send and a Ship send keep separate provenance.FIRST RELEASE

New relationships default to no automatic agent handling. Enabling a generic federation.received source is not sufficient permission to answer every stranger. Receiving a message, notifying a human, admitting agent attention, and creating an actual promise are separate decisions.

Example support grant: read this support thread, read explicitly published GSV help material, use attachments shared into this exchange, and reply to this participant. It grants no access to other reports, private conversations, credentials, connected machines, or arbitrary network endpoints.

Implementation: scoped Process authority and context isolation

Conversation history permits the signed-in human or canonical personal Ship. Explicit delegated work uses Kernel-owned authority records bound to owner, Process, allowed operations, conversations, recipients, exact resources/targets, expiry, budget, and policy revision. Scope is persisted in the process registry and trusted process-peer context, not in a model-supplied argument.

effective authority = account capabilities
                    ∩ Process restrictions
                    ∩ resource / conversation / recipient grants

approval policy applies only within that authority

Enforce scope in the owning handlers, including nested Shell and CodeMode paths, filesystem access, resource transfer, search snippets, IPC, spawning/forking, configuration, credentials, and MCP calls. An “Allow” approval cannot create missing authority. Descendants only inherit or narrow their parent's scope; changing runAs cannot escape it. Bind grants to current relationship generation and recheck policy revision at each effect.

Use a fresh context built from the explicitly granted material. Do not fork personal Ship history or private standing context into delegated work. Filter target, skill, MCP, responsibility, and resource discovery to the same scope. Do not retain another person's report in reusable memory. A summary of remote text keeps its attribution and untrusted status.

Reuse ordinary Processes, cancellation, and r12y. Accepted work may create a scoped assignment in the existing ledger; a received stranger message is not itself a promise. Expiry or revocation cancels new work and fences late replies/resource reads. An already committed outbound delivery cannot be unsent; its state remains inspectable.

Record causal message references for any Process-produced reply and persist the scoped participation budget. Acknowledgements do not trigger automatic acknowledgements. Exhaustion ends that delegated work for owner review. It does not add a universal elapsed-time or tool-call limit to unrelated personal work.

Jev and all request-to-permission classification are explicitly deferred. Deterministic admission and Kernel permissions must be sufficient. The fixed model tool surface remains Read, Write, Edit, Delete, Search, Shell, CodeMode, and Send; social operations are ordinary syscalls/commands beneath it. This document does not authorize edits to protected production prompts.

07 / OWNERSHIP AND PERSISTENCE

Small authority records. Separate message stores.

The existing architecture already separates the installation directory, per-space Kernel, per-conversation history, per-Process execution, and R2 content. Extend those ownership boundaries. There is no shared social database containing every user's conversations or contact graph.

Do not attempt distributed SQL transactions across DOs. Commit source state and a durable projection intent together, then retry idempotent destination work until its receipt is stored.

Profile projections and report evidence get distinct owned prefixes. Resource identifiers are opaque locators resolved through grants, never arbitrary physical keys accepted from a peer.

Storage growth has explicit limits

Current Conversation code archives batches of 500 when its hot message count exceeds 1,000, and history requests are bounded to 200 messages. That limits hot message bodies, not all state: permanent append receipts, reference mappings, archive manifests, r12y transitions, and transport metadata still grow.

On Workers Paid, a SQLite Durable Object has a 10 GB storage limit and executes on a single thread. Many spaces/conversations distribute across objects; one popular space's Kernel remains a concentration point. These are architecture constraints, not measured GSV capacity. Cloudflare limits.

R2 has no published per-bucket total storage or object-count ceiling, but storage, requests, and indexing still cost money. Retain immutable segments and stream bounded resources; do not append a growing transcript into one shared object. R2 limits.

Implementation: quotas, retention, capacity, and expensive recipients

Budget rows and bytes separately. Admission caps cover sender, recipient account, installation, pending approaches, transport backlog, shared assertions, resources, and automatic inference. A new sender key cannot reset a recipient-wide budget. A shared hosting suffix such as gsv.space is not one person's identity or an appropriate default block scope.

Measure admission latency, Kernel queueing, SQL bytes/rows, archive work, index lag, retained receipts, and per-source contribution. Move bounded projection/index work off the Kernel first. If legitimate traffic later saturates one Kernel, extracting partitioned delivery workers requires explicit generation/policy fencing; this RFC does not claim that adding conversation objects alone solves that bottleneck.

Conversation search does not call Exa, a web-search provider, or a model. SQLite FTS5 is available in Durable Objects, so ordinary lexical search can remain within the installation's storage boundary. SQLite/FTS5 support.

The agreed first implementation searches one selected conversation. Address-book filtering remains a separate Kernel metadata query. Account-wide message search is deferred; the UI must also show any incomplete historical indexing rather than silently presenting recent-only matches as complete history.

Implementation: one conversation, one local index
  1. Use the existing owner. Add an FTS5 index to Conversation SQLite through a numbered migration. Store searchable text and a message locator; canonical messages and immutable archive segments remain the source of truth.
  2. Commit new text locally. Insert the index row in the message append transaction. Idempotent append receipts prevent duplicate entries. No cross-DO indexing queue or acknowledgement protocol is needed.
  3. Preserve archival coverage. Keep index entries when hot message bodies move to R2. Backfill older history in bounded batches with a durable cursor in the same Conversation; new appends continue normally.
  4. Authorize the chosen conversation. The Kernel checks the caller's current access before invoking that Conversation's search. A scoped Process cannot query a different conversation. Results use canonical local message IDs and sequences.
  5. Expose coverage and limits. Return a stable continuation cursor and explicit historical coverage/backfill state. A rebuilding or capacity-limited index is not “zero results.” Deletion follows the owning Conversation's existing lifecycle.

A conversation.search syscall takes a required conversation ID and a bounded text query. Use parameterized lexical matching, newest-first results, and bounded terms, page sizes, snippets, and response bytes. This backs clients, commands, and CodeMode without expanding the fixed model tool list.

This release has no whole-inbox query, aggregate ranking, owner-index partitions, or fan-out across conversations. If those become important, measure the need before introducing another storage owner. Keep explicit row/byte budgets below the existing Conversation object's limit; report incomplete coverage instead of adding automatic sharding for a secondary feature.

Historical indexing must not scan every R2 segment during a search request. Reuse the Conversation's owned background maintenance and durable cursor. Search never implicitly downloads attachments, performs OCR, or calls an embedding, model, or external search provider.

No contact-network crawl is involved. Shared-assertion lookup remains an indexed local metadata query, and profile lookup resolves only the address the person chose.

One authority boundary, several presentations.

Do not silently redefine conversation.send: today it submits human input to an interactive local handler. Federated outbound messages use contact.send and the corresponding Process delivery route. Public SDK types own the contracts; Kernel handlers own authorization. CLI/native commands and CodeMode call those same handlers. The web UI keeps gateway/domain logic in browser services. The public profile route renders a small sanitized page without requiring the entire signed-in Instrument application. “Message with my GSV” carries only the destination profile locator to the visitor's selected space. Authentication and send confirmation happen at that space. No credentials, setup capabilities, tokens, private drafts, or account enumeration travel in cross-origin URLs. Return destinations are validated; a profile URL is data, never a redirect authority or bearer invitation.

Implementation: admission, wire versions, retries, and consistency
  1. Resolve the destination. Use the required trusted directory to resolve the HTTP hostname and current lifecycle before addressing a Kernel. Reject unknown wildcard hosts without allocating DO state. Public input never selects an installation ID. Every deployment follows this model; there is no standalone singleton exception.
  2. Bound work before admission. Enforce request size, method, endpoint, parsing depth/counts, and inexpensive abuse controls. Unpaired identity resolution itself has fetch/time/concurrency budgets.
  3. Authenticate the operation. An unpaired approach is signed by the existing Ship identity, names the intended recipient and sending subject, and binds version, operation kind, canonical destination, delivery/approach ID, nonce/validity window, and payload fingerprint. Self-certification establishes control of that identity, not a unique human or good intent.
  4. Apply local policy. Map the recipient subject to a local account from owned state, then check profile intake, actor block, quotas, current generation when applicable, and installation status. Known pinned identities must not be replaced by a newly supplied document.
  5. Commit durable intent. Record the accepted operation and stable replay outcome in Kernel SQL before asynchronous projection. The receiver, not the peer, chooses all local IDs.
  6. Complete the canonical projection. Append idempotently to the owned Conversation and update the directory/request projection. A delivered receipt is exposed only after the required durable projections are recoverable and committed. Historical search backfill and notifications do not delay that receipt; the small index entry for a new message commits in its existing Conversation append transaction.
  7. Schedule independent effects. Human notifications, permitted attention, replication of shared assertions, and delivery retries are durable bounded jobs with generation/policy checks.

Continue established pairing's current cryptographic primitives and HTTPS carrier. Normal paired v1 traffic is HMAC-authenticated with the relationship secret. V2 uses explicit domain/version binding and strict typed payloads; it does not silently reinterpret a v1 signature or widen a grant. Public documents, initial approaches, and deliberate shareable disclosure proofs use existing identity signatures. No custom cryptographic algorithm or universal remote-syscall protocol is introduced.

The current v1 document advertises a fixed protocol tuple and v1 envelopes reject unknown fields. Serve v1 and v2 documents separately during the supported upgrade window. New peers negotiate required features with the pinned counterpart and record that agreement per relationship. A missing endpoint may select a documented legacy mode; an invalid signature, identity mismatch, or malformed response must never cause a fallback that weakens authentication.

Preserve existing pairing, messages, and resource transfer with old peers where contracts remain meaningful. V1 authorship stays unspecified. Public approaches, consent-bearing shared connections, v2 work settlement, and other unsupported features remain unavailable with a clear explanation. The exact supported release window must be declared before rollout; compatibility code is removed only through that explicit upgrade policy.

No network call occurs inside a synchronous SQL transaction. Persist before I/O, retain the same idempotency identity on ambiguous retry, compare fingerprints, and recover across eviction. A delayed response can update only its still-current intent/generation. Terminal delivery failures become inspectable local state; they do not fabricate successful acceptance or silently lose a message.

Two participants' Conversations have independent sequences, retention, and histories. Federation exchanges operations and receipts, not a replicated transcript database. Changes to one local view, such as marking read or archiving, do not change the other participant's state.

10 / PRIVACY, ABUSE, AND FAILURE

Local controls must change actual processing.

Authentication answers who controls a key. Relationship policy answers what this owner accepts from that actor. Neither proves a message is truthful or safe to act on. Incoming messages, profile text, filenames, recommendations, and remote summaries remain untrusted content.

Personal control

The Kernel enforces blocks, audience restrictions, grants, resource scope, and attention policy. A quiet block sends no dedicated block notification, though network behavior may allow inference. A new generation cannot bypass an actor block.

Operator moderation

An operator may restrict abuse on its service and accept explicitly submitted reports. This is separate from a user's preferences. Independent installations use the public contracts without requiring our central moderation provider.

Current federation provides authenticated transport over HTTPS and application-readable content. It is not device-to-device encryption that excludes the hosting operator. Normal shared-secret message authentication is not independent proof to an outside moderator: both peers hold the MAC key. Reports are attributed submissions with selected evidence, not cryptographic verdicts.

Implementation: revocation, retention, reset, and deletion
  • Mute: commit the local attention policy revision; suppress future automatic wake/notification for that scope. Do not delete history or silently stop an already accepted separate responsibility.
  • Block/revoke: atomically update policy/generation, invalidate grants and queued social effects, and fence delayed sends/reads. Revocation notification is best effort. An already delivered message or completed external action cannot be recalled.
  • Unpublish: stop new public resolution and invalidate serving projections/caches. Existing accepted contacts and retained messages continue under their own policy unless explicitly revoked.
  • Unsubscribe/withdraw: immediately remove the local active assertion projection; deliver tombstones to known previous recipients and let finite leases bound offline display. Do not pretend remote copies can be erased.
  • Archive/remove contact: alter the local view/address book, preserving history and separate authorization state. The UI names these effects explicitly.
  • Account removal or installation deletion: include social rows, Conversation objects and their local indexes, publication/cache objects, attachments, shared projections, and report copies in the existing durable deletion inventory. Quiesce writers first and require completion from every owning component before reporting physical cleanup.
  • Space restriction/reset: Accounts remains authoritative. Only active installations admit ordinary work; scheduled/indexing/automation jobs recheck lifecycle. A reset creates a new installation and cannot inherit the previous social identity through its reused handle.

Retained local history is private to its authorized account and permitted Processes. Derived indexes must follow the same deletion and visibility lifecycle. Any later per-message deletion feature must cover hot SQL, immutable archive replacement/retirement, attachment references, search, Process contexts/summaries, caches, and backups with truthful retention disclosure. Hiding a row alone is not an erasure feature.

Public profiles and recommendations may be copied by viewers. Private conversation resources may be explicitly imported by a recipient. Revocation prevents future authorized access; it cannot guarantee forgetting. Future E2EE requires deciding whether the cloud Ship is a decrypting participant and which providers receive plaintext, then adopting an established protocol for that endpoint model.

Implementation: network safety, abuse budgets, reporting, and telemetry

Profile and identity resolution add externally controlled fetch destinations. Require HTTPS in production, reject userinfo and ambiguous hosts/ports, disable redirects, bound document size/time, validate the signed origin, and reject loopback/private/link-local/metadata destinations. Local development exceptions are explicit configuration, not a hostname heuristic.

Destination restrictions must apply to the actual connection, including DNS changes. A DNS precheck followed by an unconstrained fetch is insufficient. The concrete Workers egress enforcement mechanism is a release gate: confirm a runtime-supported guarantee or use an enforceable egress boundary. The current source review identified a hardening gap; it did not demonstrate internal-network access.

Unknown approaches do not auto-fetch avatars, attachments, or linked pages. Public profile media uses deliberately published, bounded immutable images and a controlled serving path. Render remote text as text or a constrained safe format; no arbitrary scripts, CSS, inline HTML, privileged widgets, or interpretation of shared text as instructions.

Enforce deterministic admission before model work. Rate limiting applies to authenticated actor, recipient account, and installation; unauthenticated request limits additionally protect parsing and identity fetches. Repeated or rotating identities cannot bypass aggregate recipient budgets. Keep separate pending-intake and established-conversation budgets, with fair scheduling and retry-after behavior that does not reveal private policy details.

Reports require a named destination and an evidence preview. Copy only selected messages/resources the reporter can disclose; reference the exact revisions. Private block/mute lists are not exported. A managed moderation service may return an enforcement outcome under its own policy, while personal warnings remain attributable claims. Reporting does not automatically publish a shared safety advisory.

Telemetry is allowlisted: outcome codes, latency stages, byte counts, quota rejection, retry counts, projection lag, object size, and cleanup progress. Never log message bodies, private aliases, prompts, tool arguments, filenames, invitation codes, raw authentication material, report evidence, or private social edges. Operational metrics are not a shadow social graph.

11 / BUILDING AND SHIPPING IT

Deliver complete slices with explicit boundaries.

The specification covers the full intended model. Implementation is staged so the first public profile cannot expose an unbounded inbox or an unrestricted personal agent. Each batch has a reviewable invariant and cross-boundary acceptance cases.

0 · Correct today's federation

Enforce work-request participant roles at local and inbound handlers. Expose unresolved delivery/settlement honestly. Preserve idempotency, resource ownership, generation fences, and current conversations. This can ship independently of social UI.

1 · Contracts and human-only conversations

Add v2 negotiation, profile/approach/relationship types, origin message references, provenance, private view state, and optional contact-conversation handlers. Define the peer-bound acceptance handshake and safe legacy behavior. Add migrations and central authorization before UI callers.

2 · First social release

Publish /@username profiles; pair privately or approach publicly; accept/decline; exchange text/resources; browse saved contacts and inbox; reply; synchronize private unread state; mute/archive/block/report; inspect delivery/retry. Ship attention remains an explicit per-contact choice. No automatic public support Process.

3 · Local relationship context and retrieval

Add bilateral disclosure consent, shared connections, attributed recommendations/advisories, bounded subscriptions and withdrawals, and deliberate introductions. Build lexical search inside each Conversation with visible archival coverage. Deliver the agreed batches as separate commits in one feature branch and one complete-feature PR.

4 · Scoped assistance

Add generic Process scope propagation and context isolation across handlers, Shell, CodeMode, search, resources, and descendants. Enable exact draft approvals and optional support automation only after these boundaries are enforced. Reuse ordinary r12y for accepted promises.

The founder trial uses ordinary product behavior

The optional “contact the founder” entry points to the founder's published profile. A user chooses whether to approach and what diagnostic information to share. The founder receives a normal request, can accept/block/report normally, and gains no special rights over the sender's space. A later explicitly scoped Process can work from selected GSV material or diagnostics, then escalate to the founder. Opt-in follow-ups use ordinary responsibilities and messages.

Acceptance cases for implementation and human trial

These are requirements for later implementation, not claims that checks have run. This document was prepared from source inspection and primary documentation. The user owns local functional verification; no tests, builds, browser sessions, live federation probes, or model calls were run.

  1. Two independently routed installations, each with multiple local accounts, publish selected profiles without leaking other accounts or My spaces ownership. Unknown hosts and unpublished subjects allocate no unauthorized state.
  2. A private invite pairs exactly the intended participants once; expired/cancelled/replayed invitations and delayed superseded pairing responses cannot change the active relationship.
  3. A public first message appears once in Requests without a Process, inference, external media fetch, or accepted promise. Accepting preserves its identity; decline/block/withdraw races remain truthful.
  4. Human and Process provenance, reply references, resources, optimistic send reconciliation, and delivery failures survive retries, archive reads, reconnects, and Process replacement.
  5. Read position synchronizes across the owner's devices without producing a remote human-read receipt or advancing on agent reads. Archive, mute, remove, revoke, and block have their distinct specified effects.
  6. A requester cannot accept its own offer using UI, SDK, Shell, CodeMode, or raw federation. Crossed acceptance/withdrawal converges or stays explicitly pending; no false mutual agreement.
  7. A resource reference exposes only the authorized immutable revision. Revocation, cancellation, wrong generation, byte mismatch, or expiry stops future access without claiming to erase an imported copy.
  8. A shared connection appears only for the permitted audience with both approvals. Hidden edges do not affect visible counts. Withdrawal, audience changes, lost deltas, expired leases, and offline recipients cannot leave an indefinitely active assertion.
  9. A scoped Process cannot read another conversation, search its snippets, send elsewhere, inherit broader context, use another target, or escape via a child, Shell, CodeMode, IPC, or MCP. Late output after revocation has no effect.
  10. Search within one authorized conversation finds eligible hot and indexed archived messages; partial historical coverage is explicit. Rebuilds and stale authorization never expose forbidden text. No cross-conversation query or additional Search DO is required.
  11. Oversized inputs, redirect/DNS tricks, new-key floods, backlogs, full budgets, and unreachable peers remain bounded and preserve ordinary owner access/cancellation.
  12. Supported v1/v2 combinations display only meaningful capabilities; unsupported security semantics fail clearly. Installation restriction, reset, and deletion cover every new owner and derived copy.

Implement meaningful regression tests at the owning boundaries and both ends of cross-boundary changes. CI can run the approved checks. Provide a clean two-space human trial with failure/retry scenarios and a visible list of unresolved limitations before enabling public intake.
